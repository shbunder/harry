# Requirements — FEAT-260918-4065d5 A Nearby section brings Oostende, Leuven and Holsbeek onto the page

## Context & problem

The morning page carries Belgium, abroad, technology, culture, basketball and one oddity. It
carries nothing about the three places its reader actually lives in and cares about:
**Oostende, Leuven and Holsbeek.**

National news reaches those towns only when something large happens in them. Measured on
2026-09-18, VRT NWS's national feed carried two such stories in fifty — a governor sent to
Oostende, and a Leuven hotel school's cheap lunch. Holsbeek, a municipality of about ten
thousand, will not appear in a national feed in a normal week at all.

So this is two problems, not one. There is no section for local news, and there are no
sources that carry it.

## Goals

- A seventh topic, `regional`, that Claude assigns like any other, printed as **Nearby** and
  running near the back — after basketball, before the oddity. The towns are a standing
  interest rather than the day's news, so the front of the paper is what happened and the back
  is what this reader keeps an eye on.
- A local paper filtered to the three towns, capped and handed over last, so one that files
  fifty stories a day cannot take the page.
- A nearby story leads the front page only when another paper carries it too.
- Sources that actually carry the three towns, so the section has something to hold.
- The section behaves like `sport` on a quiet day: absent, not padded.

## Non-goals

- **Deciding what a story is about.** Which stories are local is Claude's judgement on the
  morning, the same as every other topic. Harry does match town names, but only to decide
  *which candidates from a local paper are worth handing over* — never to assign a topic. The
  line is drawn in [[ADR-260918-713fa1]].
- A fourth or fifth place. Three towns, named in the brief, changed by editing the brief.
- Reading a local paper that blocks scripted clients. Nieuwsblad answers 403 and stays out.
- Changing how any existing topic is chosen, ordered or drawn.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: A seventh topic prints on the front page, behind the day's news

```gherkin
Given a page built with a "regional" pick, a "belgium" pick and one whose topic is unknown
When the front page is rendered
Then the regional pick carries the Nearby mark, which the unknown topic does not get
And it sorts behind the "belgium" pick and ahead of the unknown one
```

### Scenario 1c: A nearby story leads only when another paper carries it

```gherkin
Given a front-page pick whose topic is "regional" and whose "also" is empty
When the page is built
Then it is refused, naming that pick
And the same pick with an "also" builds normally
```

### Scenario 1b: Nearby has its own section on the second sheet

`picks` is the front page and `more` is the second sheet, so the heading needs a `more` entry
rather than a pick.

```gherkin
Given a page built with a "more" entry whose topic is "regional" and one whose topic is "belgium"
When the second sheet is rendered
Then it is headed "Nearby", below the "At home" section
```

### Scenario 2: The mark is the topic's own, not a fallback

```gherkin
Given a pick whose topic is "regional"
When the page is rendered
Then it is drawn with the Nearby mark rather than sorted last with no mark,
     which is what an unknown topic gets
```

### Scenario 3: A quiet day leaves the section out

```gherkin
Given a page built with no pick and no "more" entry whose topic is "regional"
When the page is rendered
Then no "Nearby" heading appears on either sheet
And every other section renders as it did before
```

### Scenario 4: The regional feeds are read alongside the national ones

```gherkin
Given the news connector is configured with rob=ROB tv and kw=KW West-Vlaanderen
      — both RSS 2.0, measured 2026-09-18 — beside vrt, bbc and tijd
When digest_list_candidates is called
Then candidates carry those feeds' slugs, so an id says which feed it came from
```

### Scenario 5: A regional feed that is down costs only itself

```gherkin
Given ROB tv answers 500 and the other feeds answer normally
When digest_list_candidates is called
Then the national headlines still arrive
And "unavailable" names ROB tv, so a thin Nearby section is not mistaken for a quiet week
```

### Scenario 6: The brief names the three towns, in both copies

```gherkin
Given the brief is changed to describe the regional topic
When the job's brief and the claude.ai routine's copy are compared
Then both carry the same three town names and the same rule for a day with none
```

### Scenario 7: Claude is handed only what it chooses on

Measured on 2026-09-18, a 40-headline payload is 21,959 characters. `image` is 14.3% of it
and `feed` 2.8%, and Claude returns neither — `digest_build` re-resolves the picture from its
own re-read of the feeds. Three more feeds make the payload matter more, not less.

```gherkin
Given digest_list_candidates is called for 40 headlines
When the answer is read
Then no headline carries "image" or "feed"
And every headline still carries "date", because the newest 40 can include yesterday's stories
And news_search still returns both, for a caller asking across days
```

## When it degrades

**A regional feed is unreachable.** The connector lists the failed feed in `unavailable` and
carries on, so the national headlines still arrive and the page prints — Scenario 5. Slack
gets one line per source, at most once per 24 hours: `ROB tv: ROB tv answered 404`. Nothing
prints on the page itself; a dead news feed produces no "unavailable" line for the reader,
unlike the weather.

That is the gap this feature has to close. **A Nearby section with one story looks exactly
like a quiet week in Oostende**, and the brief currently tells Claude to stay quiet about it:

```
- **A source says unavailable.** Choose from what did arrive and carry on. Say so in the
  intro only if it cost you a whole topic.
```

One regional feed down out of two does not cost a whole topic, so that rule keeps the
reader uninformed in precisely the case that matters. `digest_list_candidates`'s own
description already says the stronger thing — *"If `unavailable` has an entry, say so"* — so
the brief and the tool disagree today. **The brief changes to say a missing source is named
in the intro whenever the section it feeds is thin**, and the two are reconciled.

**The brief's second copy drifts.** The routine on claude.ai carries its own copy, because
claude.ai cannot reach an MCP prompt. If only one copy gains the new topic, Claude never
emits `regional`, the Nearby section never appears, nothing errors, and the page is
indistinguishable from a run of quiet weeks. **This is the most likely failure in the whole
feature and the only one that is silent and permanent.** It is the same argument the watchdog
exists for: a thing that does not happen cannot report its own absence. Nothing automated can
catch it, so it is held by the comment at the top of `JOB.md` and by Scenario 6 comparing the
two copies at close.

**KW carries a province, not a town.** Most of what it brings is not about Oostende, and
that is not a failure — Claude picks from candidates, so the rest simply is not picked. The
cost is real but quiet: fifty mostly-irrelevant headlines compete for the newest forty, so a
busy day in West Flanders can push a Leuven story out of the list before anyone chooses.

**No regional story on a given day.** The section is left out, exactly as `sport` is on a day
with no basketball. This is the common case, not an error, and nothing is reported.

## Out of scope

- **HLN**, which works and is the only source both current and actually about Oostende — six
  of its items reached the newest forty on the day it was measured. Left out by choice. What
  it costs is Oostende coverage, which now arrives only when KW or VRT happens to carry it.
- **Focus-WTV**, whose Oostende feed is exactly on topic and stopped in March 2014.
- **Stad Oostende**, which stamps all 146 items with the moment of the fetch, so every one
  would sort as "just now" and take the whole of the newest forty every morning.
- **Nieuwsblad**, which answers 403 to a scripted client.
- **VRT's own regional feeds**, which answer 410 Gone and are not coming back.
- Trimming `summary`, at 34% the largest field. It is what Claude actually reads to choose,
  and `detail="concise"` already cuts it to 280 characters.
- Raising the 40-headline default. The pool the tool *asks* for did have to grow — see
  Scenario 7 — but what Claude is handed is still 40.
