# Requirements — FEAT-260919-e9cab2 The morning page says what the late evening will do, and when the sun rises and sets

## Context & problem

The weather panel on the front sheet shows four hours: 08:00, 12:00, 16:00 and 20:00. The
last one is early evening. Whether it is still warm enough to sit outside at eleven, or
whether the rain arrives after supper, is not on the page.

The weather connector could say. Open-Meteo already sends all twenty-four hours on the call
Harry makes every morning, and the connector throws away everything after 22:00. The strip
then picks its four hours by position in the list (`[2::4][:4]` in
`.harry/tools/digest_build/tool.py`), so the hours on the page depend on where the list
happens to start.

Sunrise and sunset are not on the page at all. Open-Meteo answers both on that same call,
as two more daily fields, if they are asked for. Measured on 19 September 2026 for Leuven:
`"sunrise": ["2026-09-19T07:22"]`, `"sunset": ["2026-09-19T19:46"]`, local time because
the request already names `Europe/Brussels`.

## Goals

- The hourly strip on the front sheet adds 23:00 as a fifth hour
- The panel says when the sun rises and sets today
- Both ride on the one request Harry already makes. No second call

## Non-goals

- Tomorrow's weather, or tonight after 23:00
- Every hour on the strip. Five is still a glance; eighteen is a table
- Day length, twilight, moon, wind
- Telling Claude to mention sunset in the intro. `JOB.md` is not changed
- Changing `today()`. It stays the four facts it has always been

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The strip shows five hours, ending at 23:00

```gherkin
Given a forecast whose hours include 08:00, 12:00, 16:00, 20:00 and 23:00
When the morning page is built
Then the strip on the front sheet shows 08:00, 12:00, 16:00, 20:00 and 23:00, in that order
And each carries its own temperature and icon
And the hours are chosen by their clock time, not by their place in the list
```

### Scenario 2: The fifth hour fits inside the page

```gherkin
Given the strip shows five hours
When the PDF is read back
Then the 23:00 label ends inside the 34pt right margin
And the page reports nothing crowded
```

### Scenario 3: The forecast runs to 23:00

```gherkin
Given Open-Meteo answers twenty-four hours
When the connector reads them
Then `hours` carries eighteen readings, 06:00 to 23:00
And 05:00 and earlier are still left off
```

### Scenario 4: The forecast says when the sun rises and sets

```gherkin
Given Open-Meteo answers `sunrise` and `sunset` for today
When the connector reads them
Then the forecast carries `sunrise` and `sunset` as local "HH:MM" strings, such as "07:22" and "19:46"
And the request asks for them in its `daily` list, on the same single call
And both keys are always present: each is null when it cannot be read, and the other is unaffected
```

### Scenario 5: The page prints sunrise and sunset

```gherkin
Given a forecast with sunrise "07:22" and sunset "19:46"
When the morning page is built
Then the weather panel reads "Sunrise 07:22 · Sunset 19:46"
And with only one of the two, the panel prints that one on its own
```

### Scenario 6: Claude can ask for it

```gherkin
Given the `weather_forecast` tool
When Claude calls it
Then the answer carries `sunrise`, `sunset` and the eighteen hours
And its `TOOL.md` says so, and no longer says it is not for sunrise
```

### Scenario 7: The panel names the place it was set to

```gherkin
Given the weather connector is set to PLACE=Ghent
When the morning page is built
Then the weather panel says Ghent, not Leuven
```

`page.py` prints the word `Leuven` whatever the setting says. The line this feature adds
sunrise and sunset to is the line that carries it.

## When it degrades

| What happens | What the reader sees | What reaches Slack |
|---|---|---|
| The answer has neither `sunrise` nor `sunset`, or neither can be read as a time | Both are null in the forecast. No sun line. The high, low, rain chance and strip all print. One INFO line in the log naming what was missing | Nothing |
| Only one of the two can be read | That one prints on its own, and the INFO line names the other | Nothing |
| The hourly block stops before 23:00 | The strip shows whichever of 08:00, 12:00, 16:00, 20:00 and 23:00 the forecast has. No other hour takes 23:00's place, and there is no empty slot | Nothing |
| Open-Meteo is down, or there is no weather connector | The panel draws a cloud, `–°`, `High –° · Low –°` and `Rain –%`, with no place, no sun line and an empty strip. The rest of the page renders | Nothing — weather is the deliberate exception |

A missing sunrise must not cost the forecast. The connector reads the four day facts
strictly, so a field read the same way would turn a missing sunrise into no forecast at all.
It is read the way `hours` is read: softly, on its own.

**Why a missing sunrise reaches no one.** The day arrived in the same answer, so Open-Meteo
is working; a missing sunrise is a lost line, not a lapsed source. Weather has no credential
to lapse either, which is why it is the one source that never alerts.

The last row is what the page does today, and the place is no longer printed there because
nothing supplies one. Harry's docs say this case prints `Weather unavailable`; nothing does.
That gap is older than this feature and is tracked on its own.

## Out of scope

- Places where the sun does not rise or set on a given day. Harry is set to one place in
  Belgium. An answer there that cannot be read falls into the first row above
- Choosing which hours the strip shows from a setting. Five fixed hours
- Re-recording the existing weather fixtures. A new recording carries sunrise and sunset;
  the old ones stay as the "answered without them" case
