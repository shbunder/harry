# Requirements — FEAT-260912-24d052 Headlines arrive from VRT NWS and the BBC

## Context & problem

The morning page is Claude choosing six stories out of forty. This is the forty.

Two public feeds, no credential between them: VRT NWS for Belgium and the BBC for the
world. Harry fetches both, turns them into a flat list of candidates, and fetches the full
text of the ones Claude picks. Nothing here ranks, filters by importance, or summarises —
that is the judgement the whole design keeps on Claude's side.

Three things about the real feeds made this harder than "parse some RSS", and all three
were found by downloading them rather than by reasoning about them:

- **The two feeds are different formats.** The BBC serves RSS 2.0 with `<channel>` and
  `<item>`. VRT NWS serves Atom — a namespaced `<feed>` of `<entry>` elements. A parser
  that reads only one of them reads zero VRT headlines and reports nothing wrong, because
  "no items" and "an empty feed" look identical.
- **The same story appears twice with two different titles.** `feeds.bbci.co.uk/news/rss.xml`
  and `.../news/world/rss.xml` both carried `bbc.co.uk/news/videos/cx2z5gjj838o` on
  14 September 2026, once as *"Watch: Why Russian strike on train could be sign of
  escalation"* and once as *"Watch: Why a Russian strike on train near Ukraine-Poland border
  could mark an escalation"*. Deduplicating on the headline would have kept both.
- **VRT's obvious link is not the article.** Each Atom entry has two links. `rel="self"`
  points at a per-article `.rss.xml` document — the feed entry again, not the story.
  `rel="alternate"` is a short `vrtnws.be/p.oL1bKEomY` URL that redirects to the article and
  extracts to 2,208 characters of Dutch prose.

The failure this feature has to avoid is the quiet one. A dead VRT feed does not look
broken: the candidate list is just shorter, Claude picks six BBC stories, and the page
reads like a slow news day in Belgium. Weather can stay silent when it breaks because the
page says `Weather unavailable` in the place the forecast should be. News has no such
place. **So a source that fails has to say so in Slack.**

## Goals

- Claude can ask for today's headlines and get one list, newest first, from both feeds.
- The same story carried by two feeds appears once.
- Every candidate has an id a person can read, and that id fetches the article later.
- Claude can read the full text of a story it picked.
- One dead feed costs its own headlines and nothing else, and reaches Slack.
- Every parsing test runs against a feed recorded in `tests/fixtures/news/`.

## Non-goals

- **De Tijd.** It blocks scripted clients and needs a browser session, which is
  FEAT-260912-9c933f.
- **Ranking, scoring or filtering by importance.** Harry returns what the feeds carried.
  Choosing is Claude's, and that is [[ADR-260912-bd36c2]].
- **Summarising an article.** `summary` is the description the feed supplied, verbatim.
- Categories, tags, images, or an archive of past days.
- **Retrying a feed.** One attempt, 10 seconds, and the next call is a fresh one. A feed
  that is down stays down for longer than a retry loop is willing to wait, and the morning
  page cannot afford to spend its build time discovering that.
- Full text for anything that is not on a configured feed.

## What this offers the digest

FEAT-260912-0f2744 wrote two signatures down before any source existed. Both are kept:

| The digest calls | It gets back |
|---|---|
| `news.candidates(limit)` | `[{id, title, source, feed, published, date, summary, link}]`, newest first |
| `news.article(id)` | `{available, id, title, source, published, link, text}` — raises on an id it does not know |

**`candidates()` is still a plain list.** It cannot report a dead feed, and it is not asked
to: the morning page learns about that from Slack, and `news_search` — which is a different
caller with a different need — gets `unavailable` alongside its candidates.

Two changes to what `docs/sources.md` records today, both additive:

- A candidate carries `link`, `feed` and `date` as well as the five fields already written
  down. `feed` is the slug you filter by, `date` is the local day the id is built from, and
  `link` is what makes a headline clickable on a page.
- `article()` carries `available`. On a page that will not load it is `false`, there is no
  `text`, and `why` says what happened — **the headline, source and time are still there**,
  so a digest can print the story and say the text could not be read.

`docs/sources.md` is updated to match in the same change.

## How a feed is configured

One setting, `FEEDS`, holding every feed. Adding a third is a line in `.env.local`, never
a code change:

```
FEEDS=vrt=VRT NWS=https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl.rss.articles.xml|bbc=BBC News=https://feeds.bbci.co.uk/news/rss.xml
```

Feeds are separated by `|`. Each one is `slug=Name=url`, split on the first two `=` so a
URL may contain as many more as it likes. The **slug** starts every id from that feed. The
**Name** is what a person reads on the page and in an answer.

Both settings live in the connector's own folder, which is [[ADR-260912-895441]]:
`.harry/connectors/news/CONNECTOR.md` declares them with their defaults, `make env-template`
generates the committed `.harry/connectors/news/.env` from that declaration, and whatever
differs on this machine goes in `.harry/connectors/news/.env.local` beside it. A container
injects `HARRY_NEWS_FEEDS` instead.

A second setting, `TIMEZONE`, decides which day a story belongs to. It defaults to
`Europe/Brussels` — the same rule the weather connector already follows, so "today" means
the day where the page is read rather than in UTC. `published` itself is returned as the
feed gave it, in UTC.

## When it degrades

Every failure here is one source, never the page.

| What broke | What Claude gets | What reaches Slack |
|---|---|---|
| One feed 404s, times out after 10s, or is unreachable | The other feeds' candidates, and `unavailable: [{source, why}]` | One line naming the feed and why, once per 24 hours |
| One feed answers something that is not a feed | The same | The same |
| Every feed is down | An empty candidate list and every feed in `unavailable` | One line per feed |
| An article page is blocked or gone | `{available: false, why}` for that story | One line naming the source, once per 24 hours |
| An article page is readable but the extractor finds nothing | `{available: false, why: "no article text …"}` | The same |
| A feed declares a document type | The same as any unreadable feed | The same |

The morning page's news section is built from whatever `candidates()` returned. Fewer
stories is a shorter section, and the rest of the page is untouched.

**The 5-minute cache is a fetch cache, not a fallback.** A feed that fails is not served
from a previous success — a headline list that silently ages is worse than a short one,
because nothing on the page says how old it is.

## Out of scope

- De Tijd, and any source that needs a login or a browser.
- A stored history of candidates. Harry re-reads the feeds; there is no database here.
- Serving stale headlines when a feed is down.
- Translating anything. VRT NWS is Dutch and stays Dutch.
- An `always_load` news tool. Both tools defer and are found with `harry_find_tools`.
