# Requirements — FEAT-260912-0f2744 The morning page lands on the tablet before you're up

## Context & problem

What all of it is for. At 06:30 a Claude scheduled task asks Harry for candidates, decides
what matters, writes a short intro and asks for the page. Harry fetches the chosen articles,
renders the PDF and pushes it.

**Written before any source existed; revised now that four of the five do.** Weather,
calendar, news and the tablet are merged and answering. De Tijd is still its own feature and
the page degrades to that feed's own summary without it. What has also happened since is a
design spike — `scratch/digest-look.py` — which rendered the real page from the real sources,
was shown to its reader, and was changed five times on his notes. **Everything below the line
"What the page looks like" is what that spike settled**, and it is here rather than in a
stylesheet because the shape of the page is the product.

The page is still **supposed** to render with a section missing. A dead feed prints
"unavailable" and the rest renders. A page with no sources at all is not a stub: it is the
degraded state, which this feature has to get right anyway.

Every source is declared `optional:` — including the tablet. A page with nowhere to push is
a page on disk, which is what `make digest-dry` has always been for. See
[[ADR-260913-c477cd]].

## Goals

- Claude can ask what today could contain, choose from it, and get a page built.
- The page carries the articles Claude picked, each with the line Claude wrote about it.
- A section whose source is missing says so, and the rest renders.
- A page renders with no sources configured at all, so every later source has somewhere to
  arrive.
- The PDF is the size the tablet wants, with an outline its navigator reads.

## Non-goals

- **Building any source.** Weather, calendar, news and the tablet are merged and answering;
  De Tijd is its own feature. This one composes them and draws the page.
- Choosing, ranking or summarising anything. Claude picks and Claude writes; Harry fetches,
  renders and delivers. That boundary is the reason Harry exists in this shape.
- More than one page layout, or a way to configure the layout. The reader sets a name and
  the calendar colours; everything else about the look is decided here.
- **De Tijd's full text.** Its articles need a signed-in headed browser and a virtual display
  on the NUC — its own feature. Without it a De Tijd pick renders from the feed's summary,
  which is the degraded path this feature has to get right anyway.
- Choosing which stories are related, or what topic one belongs to. Both are on the pick.

## What a source has to offer

This is the feature that defines the interface the weather, calendar, news, De Tijd and
tablet features each have to present. Written here because all five of their requirements
pages are still empty, and a page built against an interface nobody wrote down is a page
tested against a stand-in of its own invention.

| The digest calls | It gets back |
|---|---|
| `weather.forecast()` | `{available, place, summary, high, low, rain_chance, hours}`; `hours` is seventeen `{at, temperature, summary}` |
| `calendar.today()` | `[{at, ends, title, where, calendar}]` — empty list when there is nothing on |
| `news.candidates(limit)` | `[{id, title, source, feed, published, date, summary, link, image}]`, newest first |
| `news.article(id)` | `{available, id, title, source, published, link, summary, image, text}` — raises on an id it does not know |
| `tablet.push(path, name)` | `{where, id, name}` — where it ended up, for the answer to quote |

All five are shipped and are what the connectors answer today. Three of them carry a field
this page is the only caller of: `hours` draws the strip, `ends` and `calendar` draw the
timetable in the reader's own colours, and `image` is the address of a picture the page
fetches for itself.

**The page fetches pictures; the connectors hand over addresses.** Forty candidates carry
about thirty pictures and six get printed, so downloading them to list headlines would be
3.6 MB of base64 for pictures about to be discarded — [[ADR-260915-c01ab8]]. That makes
fetching, embedding and the per-host awkwardness this feature's job: `images.tijd.be`
answers 403 to a plain request and 200 to one carrying a browser User-Agent, and a picture
that will not come costs the picture and not the story.

**`news.article(id)` is what makes the two calls independent.** The digest holds nothing
between them: `digest_build` asks the news source to resolve each id, and an id the source
does not know is that source's error to raise. There is no day's-candidates list to keep
anywhere, no cache to invalidate, and no shared state between two folders that are forbidden
from importing each other.

The stand-ins live in `tests/fixtures/capabilities/connectors/`, the way the loader's
fixtures already do. The real sources implement the same five signatures later.

## The two tools, and why two

Settled in [[ADR-260912-b22e46]] and not reopened: **Claude must see the candidates, because
choosing among them is the entire product.** Consolidating them would mean either the tool
picks the articles — which deletes the judgement Harry is built to preserve — or the tool
calls itself.

```
digest_list_candidates(limit=40, detail='concise')
  → { date, weather, agenda, headlines: [ {id, title, source, published, summary, image} ] }

digest_build(intro, picks, more)
  picks: [ {id, note, also: [id], topic} ]   the front page: six, each with a note
  more:  [ {id, also: [id], topic} ]         the second page: ten to twelve, grouped by topic
  → { pages, articles, delivered }
```

**A pick is four things, and three of them are judgement Harry must not attempt.**

- `id` — which story.
- `note` — the line Claude writes about why this article is here. It goes on the page above
  the article, and it is the half of the judgement that would otherwise be thrown away the
  moment the choice was made.
- `also` — the ids of other pieces **on the same story**. Deciding that a VRT headline and a
  BBC headline are the same story is judgement: a heuristic on shared words would merge two
  different stories about Trump on the day there are three. What Harry could reasonably add
  later is a *hint* — candidates that look related by title overlap — so Claude is not
  reading forty headlines cold. That is arithmetic and it stays a suggestion.
- `topic` — one of `belgium`, `world`, `tech`, `culture`, `sport`, `oddity`. Drawn as a small coloured
  mark under the article's number. A keyword rule would file every article mentioning a
  company under technology on the day one of them is about a court case.

`picks` is a declared type rather than a bare list of dictionaries, so the schema Claude
reads says what a pick is. A weak schema here is a tool the model gets wrong on the first
call of the morning. A pick with no `also` is a story nobody else is running; a pick with no
`topic`, or one the table does not carry, gets no mark rather than a wrong one.

`picks` is a declared type rather than a bare list of dictionaries, so the schema Claude
reads says what a pick is. A weak schema here is a tool the model gets wrong on the first
call of the morning.

**Both tools are `always_load: true`**, against the usual rule that tools defer. The job
that needs them runs every morning, and a tool-search round trip at 06:30 is a failure mode
with no upside. Two entries in the roster is cheap; a morning that fails on its first call is
not.

**The candidate order is newest first, capped at `limit`, and a capped answer says how many
were dropped.** Newest is a fact. Any order that is about importance is ranking, which is
the judgement this whole design exists to leave with Claude. `summary` is the source's own
summary, verbatim — truncated at 280 characters under `detail='concise'`, whole under
`'full'`. Harry never writes one.

## What the page looks like

Settled by a design spike that rendered the real page from the real sources and was revised
five times on its reader's notes. The first attempt was text in two columns and was rejected
in one sentence — *"not at all what I had in mind"* — so the sketch below is not a
suggestion, it is the acceptance criteria in picture form.

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ The Bundervoet Daily                          15 September 2026   │  masthead
│                                                TUESDAY · WEEK 38  │
│═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════│
│  ☁   29°     High 29° · Low 17°      09:00 13:00 17:00 21:00      │  weather
│      overcast Rain 53% · Leuven       19°   27°   29°   22°       │
│                                        ☀     ☀     ☀     ☁        │
│───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────│
│  <Claude's intro, in its own words, set across two columns>        │  intro
│                                                                   │
│  THE DAY          18 ENTRIES │ IN THIS PAGE        6 ARTICLES     │
│  ● Kids ● KBC Agenda         │ 1 ▤  Amerikaans leger bevestigt…   │
│  ● Papa - School             │      VRT NWS · 05:32               │ [img]
│  07 ┃07:00–09:00 Kids School │      │ BBC NEWS US confirms for…   │
│  08 ┃                        │ 2 ▣  Trump says AI safety fears…   │
│  09 │ 09:00–09:50 spar tijd  │      BBC NEWS · 08:49              │ [img]
│  10 │┃10:30 Sync┃10:30 EG:…  │      │ DE TIJD Trump belt Nvidia…  │
│  …                           │      │ DE TIJD China: 'Amerikaan…  │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**The masthead** carries a name the reader chooses, and the date on the masthead's own
baseline with the weekday under it. The name is a setting: this reader's is
*The Bundervoet Daily*.

**The weather panel** is an icon for the day, the high in display type, the day's numbers,
and a strip of four hours each carrying a temperature **and an icon of its own** — nine
degrees under a sun and nine under a thundercloud are different mornings. Every icon is
drawn as inline SVG: the container has no emoji font, an icon font is another dependency to
pin, and these are eight shapes that stay sharp at 229 dpi.

**The timetable is a real calendar, not a list.** A block is as tall as the time it takes,
and it is drawn in **its calendar's own colour** — the reader assigns them, and this
reader's are Shaun yellow, Jan green, Kids pink, KBC Agenda blue. A legend names only the
calendars that put something on today's page, because a colour nobody can decode is
decoration.

**Overlapping events get columns, and the columns are local.** An earlier attempt counted
the largest overlap anywhere in the day and split *every* block by it, so three meetings
colliding at 09:00 left a lone 20:00 event one third of a column wide. The reader's words:
*"only at the hour they are overlapping should columns be created"*. Columns belong to the
events that actually collide and to nobody else.

**The index** is numbered, each entry carrying a **topic mark** under its number, the
headline, the source and time, and the story's picture. Under an entry with companions sit
up to two "see also" lines, each a link; a third and beyond are counted — *"+1 more at the
end of the story"* — and appear in full at the foot of the article.

**Both columns fill the sheet.** The timetable stretches to the bottom margin and the index
carries enough stories to reach it. Neither may run past it.

**Each article gets its own page**: source and topic as a kicker, the headline, the picture,
Claude's note set apart, then the text in two columns. At the foot, the pieces on the same
story are drawn **as index rows** — number, topic mark, full headline, picture, tappable —
because a companion was chosen the same way the lead was and a line of grey text does not
read like something worth opening. Below them, two ways out: back to the lead piece, and
back to the front page. A companion's page carries *"Part of: <the lead>"* above its
headline as a third.

**Everything is navigable with a finger.** The tablet has no address bar. Every headline,
every thumbnail, every companion and every way out is a link annotation in the PDF.

**509.34 × 679.13 points**, measured in Phase 0 against a stock Mac, expressible in plain
CSS `@page`. At any other size the tablet rescales and the type goes soft. The left margin
carries **24 points more than the others**, because the tablet's own edit bar sits over that
strip.

**`picks` is capped at 12.** The brief asks for six to eight; twelve is headroom, and an
uncapped `picks` is an uncapped PDF at one to two pages each. Companions do not count
against it — they are part of a pick.

**`digest_build` emits a progress notification per article.** It is the one call here that
blocks for minutes, and Phase 0 measured the rule: a silent call is aborted at 300 seconds
and a call reporting progress every 30 survives 660. Per-article fetch timeouts belong to
each source connector's own feature, not here.

## What the reader sets, and what Claude sets

Two different things, and keeping them apart is what stops the page becoming configuration.

| Setting | What it is | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `name` | What the journal calls itself | `The Morning Page` |
| `colours` | `Shaun=yellow\|Kids=pink` — a calendar name to one of seven inks | empty, and every calendar is grey |
| `out_dir` | Where the PDF is written | `/data/digest` |

Claude sets the intro, the picks, the notes, the grouping and the topics — on the call, every
morning, never in a file. A setting is something true of every morning; anything that is
true of *this* morning is an argument.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Claude asks what today could contain

```gherkin
Given no source connectors are loaded
When digest_list_candidates() is called
Then it returns today's date, weather "unavailable", agenda "unavailable" and no headlines
And it does not raise
```

### Scenario 2: A source that is there fills its section

```gherkin
Given a connector the digest declares optional, offering today() and candidates()
When digest_list_candidates() is called
Then weather.today() is called once and its answer is the weather section
And news.candidates(limit) is called once and its answers are the headlines
And a source whose call raises leaves its own section "unavailable" and the others intact
And a source that returns None — which is how weather says it has nothing — prints
  "Weather unavailable" rather than a blank
```

The `None` half matters more than it looks. The weather connector's argument for sending no
Slack message rests on you seeing "Weather unavailable" on the page, every morning, where a
forecast belongs. If the page rendered a blank for `None`, that argument would quietly become
wrong.

One section's failure is one section. The page is the reason this rule exists.

### Scenario 3: Claude picks, and the page carries what Claude wrote

```gherkin
Given three headlines among the candidates
When digest_build(intro="...", picks=[{id, note}, {id, note}]) is called
Then the front page carries the intro verbatim
And each picked article appears with the note Claude wrote above its text
And an article whose text could not be fetched still appears, with its headline, its note,
  and a line saying the text was unavailable
And an id that is not among today's candidates is an error naming it
```

Harry never edits the intro or the notes. Editing them would be reasoning about what Claude
meant, and Claude is right there.

### Scenario 4: The page looks like a newspaper, not a list

```gherkin
Given a weather forecast with hours, a day of events across two calendars, and six picks
When digest_build is called
Then the masthead carries the configured name and the date on its own baseline
And the weather panel carries an icon for the day and four hours each with a temperature and an icon
And each event is a block as tall as its own length, in its calendar's colour
And a legend names only the calendars with something on today
And each index entry carries its number, its topic mark, its headline, its source and its picture
```

### Scenario 5: Columns appear only where events actually collide

```gherkin
Given three events overlapping at 09:00 and one event alone at 20:00
When the timetable is drawn
Then the three at 09:00 are each a third of the column wide
And the one at 20:00 is the full width of the column
```

### Scenario 6: Both columns reach the bottom of the sheet

```gherkin
Given an intro of any length and a day of any shape
When the front page is laid out
Then the timetable ends within 10 points of the bottom margin
And no part of either column crosses the bottom margin or the right margin
And the whole front page is page one
```

The intro is Claude's, so nobody knows how deep it runs until it has been set, and the
all-day band is as tall as today happens to be. A guessed constant was wrong by 40 points in
both directions on consecutive days. The sheet is therefore laid out twice: once to find
where the timetable starts, once to give it every point between there and the bottom.

### Scenario 7: A story several papers are running is one entry, not four

```gherkin
Given a pick carrying also: three other candidate ids
When digest_build is called
Then the index shows the lead entry with two "see also" lines under it, each a link
And a line saying "+1 more at the end of the story"
And all three companions appear at the foot of the lead's page, as index rows with pictures
And each companion has a page of its own, carrying "Part of: <the lead>" as a link
And every one of those is a link annotation in the PDF
```

### Scenario 8: An id that lost or gained a word still finds its story

```gherkin
Given a pick whose id is "vrt-2026-09-15-historische-prinskensmolen-in-meerhout"
And exactly one of today's candidates has an id that begins with it
When digest_build is called
Then it resolves to that candidate and the page is built
Given a pick whose id is "bbc-2026-09-15-suspected-sabotage-causes-major-netherlands-rail-disruption"
And exactly one candidate's id is a beginning of it
Then it resolves to that candidate too
And the answer lists every id that had to be resolved, with what it became
Given an id that matches no candidate either way, or matches two
Then it is an error naming the id and naming the candidates it could have meant
```

**Measured, not imagined, and measured twice.** The brief was run against a real morning's
forty headlines on Sonnet and on Haiku. Sonnet passed every mechanical rule. Haiku produced
six correct picks, the right topics and no sport on a day with no basketball — and got the
ids wrong **in both directions**: the first run *truncated* two, dropping the last word; the
second run, against a brief rewritten to warn about exactly that, *lengthened* two instead,
rebuilding them from the headline. Prose did not fix it and a third rewrite would not either.

So the rule is symmetric: **one id is a beginning of the other, and exactly one candidate
matches.** Both failures resolve under it, and on that morning's forty headlines no two
candidate ids begin one another, so the rule had nothing to be ambiguous about — which is a
property to assert rather than to assume.

This is arithmetic, not judgement: a string begins another or it does not. Refusing instead
would cost a failed build and a retry at 06:30, on the model most likely to need one.
**Harry says what it resolved** — a silent correction is how the wrong story gets printed
and nobody finds out.

### Scenario 9: A picture that will not come costs the picture

```gherkin
Given a candidate whose image address answers 403 to a plain request
When digest_build fetches it
Then it is asked for again with a browser User-Agent
And a candidate whose image cannot be fetched at all appears without one
And the story, its headline, its note and its text are unaffected
```

### Scenario 10: The page is the size the tablet wants

```gherkin
When a page is rendered
Then the PDF is 509.34 by 679.13 points
And the left margin is 24 points wider than the others, clear of the tablet's edit bar
And it has an outline entry per article, in the order Claude picked them
And the front page is page one
```

### Scenario 11: Nowhere to push is a page on disk

```gherkin
Given no tablet connector is loaded
When digest_build is called
Then the page is written to <out_dir>/<date>.pdf, out_dir defaulting to /data/digest
And the answer quotes that path, so the caller is not left guessing
When a tablet connector is loaded
Then tablet.push(path, name) is called once and the answer quotes what it returned
And the page is still on disk, because a push is a copy and not a move
```

`out_dir` is the digest's own `config:` setting, so it is overridden the way every
capability's is — `HARRY_DIGEST_BUILD_OUT_DIR` in the environment, or `.env.local` beside
the declaration. That is what lets `make digest-dry` put a page in `out/` without touching
the data volume.

### Scenario 12: The job is one markdown file

```gherkin
Given .harry/jobs/morning-page/
When a client lists the prompts
Then the brief is there, named for the job
And the brief tells Claude to call digest_list_candidates, then digest_build, then
  harry_mark_done
And nothing schedules it — a Claude task owns that clock
```

### Scenario 13: Seeing it without a tablet

```gherkin
When `make digest-dry` is run
Then a page is rendered to out/digest.pdf from whatever sources are configured
And it prints what it put on the page and what it could not
And it needs no running server, no credential and no tablet
```

The one command that answers "does the page look right".

It runs through `scripts/call_tool.py`, which loads the capabilities and calls one tool by
name with JSON arguments. Generic on purpose: `make digest-dry` names a tool in a Makefile
recipe, which is a developer convenience, and nothing in `src/harry/` learns a capability's
name. **`make digest-now` is removed.** It points at a module that has never existed, and
pushing to a tablet is a Non-goal of this feature — it comes back with the connector that
can do it.

### Scenario 14: A capability may declare a connector optional

```gherkin
Given a tool declaring `optional: [weather]`
When the weather connector did not load
Then the tool loads anyway, and context.connectors has no 'weather'
When the weather connector did load
Then the tool loads and context.connectors['weather'] is what it registered
And a tool declaring `requires: [weather]` is still skipped when weather did not load
And `make lint` refuses an `optional:` naming a connector that does not exist, the way it
  already refuses a `requires:` that does
```

The field has sat in the `/new-tool` template since the scaffolding with nothing
implementing it. [[ADR-260913-c477cd]] decided what it means, and the test for which list a
name belongs in: **if this connector is missing, is there still something worth putting on
the page?**

## When it degrades

- **No sources at all** — every section says unavailable, the page renders, and `digest-dry`
  says so. This is the state the feature ships in, so it is the best-tested path in it.
- **One source raises** — that section says unavailable. The others are untouched.
- **An article's text cannot be fetched** — the headline, the source, Claude's note and the
  feed's own summary still appear, with a line saying the full text was not available.
  Losing the text is not losing the entry: Claude chose it, and that choice is information,
  and the summary the feed wrote is real reporting.
- **A picture cannot be fetched** — the story renders without one. Nothing is retried beyond
  the one browser User-Agent retry, and nothing is said: a feed that publishes no pictures is
  a feed, not a fault.
- **A calendar has no colour assigned** — its events are drawn in grey and the legend says so.
  Colour is a convenience; a missing afternoon is not.
- **A companion piece cannot be fetched** — it keeps its page, carrying its headline and the
  feed's summary, and stays in the lead's foot. A "see also" that vanishes looks like a
  story nobody else ran.
- **Rendering fails** — the tool returns an error to Claude, which is the caller and can say
  so. Nothing alerts: the deadline watchdog already covers the case nobody hears about,
  which is the page never being built at all.
- **The push fails** — the page is still on disk and the answer says so. Retrying a push is
  the tablet connector's business, not the digest's.

**Nothing here reaches Slack, and that is a decision rather than an omission.** Each source
owns its own alert, because each one knows what its own failure means: a lapsed De Tijd
login is that connector's to report, a dead feed is the news connector's. The digest sees
only "this section is unavailable" and cannot tell a source that is broken from a source
nobody has configured — so an alert from here would fire every morning until all five
exist, which is how a channel gets muted.

Two things already cover the cases that matter. A capability that failed to load is alerted
at start-up, once per day, by the loader. And a page that was never built at all is the
deadline watchdog's, which is merged and running. What is deliberately **not** covered is a
section that was there for a month and quietly stopped: that is each source's own alert to
raise, and every source feature carries a criterion for it.

## Out of scope

- Weather, calendar, feeds, De Tijd, the tablet. Each is its own feature.
- Caching anything between the two calls beyond what one morning needs.
- More than one page a day, or rebuilding a page that was already delivered.
