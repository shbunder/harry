# Requirements — FEAT-260919-f580cd The second sheet reads like a newspaper page, and the front page fills to its foot

## Context & problem

Two things on the page the owner does not like, measured on 2026-09-19.

**The front page's "Other articles" button lands on a story.** The button is pinned to the foot
of the right-hand column by a spacer. The timetable in the left column draws its hour rows with
`position: absolute`, which gives that column no height of its own, so the right column is
stretched to a height shorter than its six stories. The stories run past the column's foot and
the button sits on top of the fifth. `fit` reported `news 371 < column 416` that morning and
thought it fitted. The same fault leaves **the bottom third of the front page empty**: the
timetable caps an hour at 34pt, so a quiet day's six hours come to 204pt of a 302pt column.

**The second sheet feels empty.** Four across made small cards; a section with two or three of
them leaves the rest of its row blank, and every card is the same size, so nothing leads the
eye. Twenty stories fitted on one page with room left over.

Four designs were rendered from one real morning and shown to the owner at
https://claude.ai/artifact/2NftDV9a2pBrCrgQt4uKwj. Mosaic was broken in WeasyPrint. Magazine
and Featured could not fill a second page on a full day — 47% and 32%. **Broadsheet** fitted a
full day of 18 on one page at 97% and a light day of 10 on one page at 97%. The owner chose it,
and set the rule every layout has to keep: **the front page, at most two index pages, then
the articles.**

## Goals

- The front page's way onward sits in the right column's head, where "6 articles" said nothing
  a reader could use, and can no longer overlap a story.
- On a quiet day the timetable shows more of the day instead of stopping short, so the left
  column reaches the foot of the page.
- The second sheet is three columns of headlines. The story that opens a run carries a picture
  and the first sentence of its summary; the rest are headline and byline.
- No section headings. The topic is the mark beside each story's source, and the paper's
  running order is kept.
- The sheet fits itself: for each day it chooses how often a story gets a picture and how tall.
  A day that fits one page gets one page, as full as it will go; a day that needs two gets two.

## Non-goals

- A mosaic, magazine or featured layout. Measured and not chosen.
- Filling the front page's right column to its foot. Six stories are what they are; the left
  column is the one that stopped short.
- Changing how the front page's six are chosen, drawn or ordered.
- Changing the article pages beyond their link to the second sheet.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The way onward is in the column head

```gherkin
Given a page built with six front-page picks and eighteen on the second sheet
When the front page is rendered
Then the right column's head carries "Other news →", linking to the second sheet
And there is no button beneath the stories
```

### Scenario 2: A page with nothing on the second sheet offers no way to it

```gherkin
Given a page built with front-page picks and no second-sheet stories
When the page is rendered
Then neither the front page nor any article page carries an "Other news" link
```

Today every article page links to `#more` whether or not a second sheet exists, so on a day
with no `more` that link goes nowhere.

### Scenario 3: A quiet day's timetable reaches the foot of its column

```gherkin
Given a day whose only timed event runs 09:30 to 11:30
And a column with more room than six hours at 34pt fill
When the timetable is drawn
Then it shows hours past 13:00, and its grid is as tall as the room it was given
And no hour is taller than 34pt
```

### Scenario 4: A busy day's timetable is not stretched

```gherkin
Given a day with timed events from 07:00 to 21:00
When the timetable is drawn
Then it shows 07 to 22 and no more, at no less than 18pt an hour
```

### Scenario 4a: A day with nothing timed still fits its column

```gherkin
Given a day with no timed events, or a calendar that could not be read
When the timetable is drawn
Then it shows 07 to 21, sized to the room it was given
And the day column stays on the front page
```

Today this case draws fifteen hours at a fixed 24pt — 360pt, against about 300pt of room.

### Scenario 4b: A day too long for the column says so

```gherkin
Given a day with timed events from 06:00 to 23:00
And a column with less room than eighteen hours at 18pt
When the page is rendered
Then the hours are drawn at 18pt, the smallest a half-hour meeting can show its name at
And the answer's `crowded` says the day column did not fit on the front sheet
```

### Scenario 5a: The story opening each run leads it

```gherkin
Given a second sheet set with a picture every k stories
When it is rendered
Then the 1st, (k+1)th, (2k+1)th … stories carry their picture, if they have one, a larger
  headline, and the first sentence of their summary, cut at a word within 120 characters
And every other story carries its headline and its source, and no picture
And every headline is printed whole
And a story's companions each get one line beneath it, as they did on the card
```

### Scenario 5: The second sheet is three columns with no section headings

```gherkin
Given eighteen second-sheet stories across five topics
When the second sheet is rendered
Then it is set in three columns
And no topic's heading appears on it
And each story carries its topic's mark
And the stories run in the paper's order: home, abroad, technology, culture, sport, nearby, the one worth knowing
```

### Scenario 6: The sheet fits itself: fewest pages, then the fullest last page

```gherkin
Given a full day of 20 second-sheet stories with pictures and full-length headlines
And a light day of 10
When each second sheet is laid out
Then each is set at the setting on the ladder with the fewest pages
And among those, the one whose last page is fullest
And neither runs past two pages
```

The ladder, from most generous to leanest, as *(a picture every k stories, picture height)*:
(2, 86pt), (2, 70pt), (3, 80pt), (3, 66pt), (4, 66pt), (4, 54pt), (5, 54pt), (6, 48pt),
(8, 44pt). Below 44pt a crop is a stripe; past eight, a column with no picture reads as a
list. Each setting is measured by rendering the sheet on its own.

**No fill floor.** The page can hold only what the day gave: three stories fill a third of a
page at any setting, and adding stories is Claude's call, not Harry's.

### Scenario 6a: A sheet that cannot fit two pages still renders, and says so

```gherkin
Given a second sheet whose leanest setting still runs to three pages
When the page is built
Then the sheet is set at the setting with the fewest pages
And the answer's `crowded` says "also today" runs to 3 pages
```

### Scenario 7: A summary that arrives as HTML prints as text

```gherkin
Given a story whose summary is KW's, as recorded in tests/fixtures/news/kw-west-vlaanderen.xml
When it opens a run on the second sheet
Then its summary line reads "Na een welverdiende pauze zijn de senioren opnieuw gestart met hun wekelijkse sportuurtje."
And the page carries no tag and no character code such as "&#8230;"
```

### Scenario 7a: A picture that will not come is asked for once

```gherkin
Given a second-sheet story whose picture answers 404
When the sheet is fitted across the ladder
Then the picture is requested once in the whole build
```

Today a failed picture is not remembered, so every render asks again, each time for up to
15 seconds.

### Scenario 8: One name for one place

```gherkin
Given an article page on a day with a second sheet
When its way back to the second sheet is drawn
Then it is called "Other news", as on the front page
```

### Scenario 9: The brief stops asking the news to fit the layout

```gherkin
Given the morning-page brief
When Claude reads step 3
Then it no longer asks for about four stories in each category
And it says a category gets what the day has
```

## When it degrades

**A day too heavy to fit two pages at the leanest setting.** Twenty stories is the most the tool
accepts, and eighteen real ones fitted one page, so this is not expected. If it happens, the
sheet renders at the setting with the fewest pages and runs onto a third; the reader turns
one page more before the articles. `crowded` in the build's answer says "also today runs to
3 pages", and the log carries it at WARNING. **Nothing reaches Slack, and the brief does not
ask Claude to act on it** — the page is whole, and a rebuild with fewer stories is not worth
a second 06:30 fetch. Scenario 6a forces it.

**A picture that will not come.** The story prints without it and the rest keep theirs. A
failed address is remembered for the rest of the build, so it costs at most one wait of
15 seconds, however many times the ladder renders the sheet — Scenario 7a. Only the stories
opening a run are fetched for the sheet, so fewer pictures are asked for than before.

**Measuring the sheet costs time.** Each setting is one render of the sheet on its own, about a
second with pictures already fetched, and there are nine. The build already blocks for a
minute or two; this adds about ten seconds.

**A calendar that is down, or a day with nothing timed.** The timetable draws 07 to 21, sized
to the room — Scenario 4a. The calendar's own failure is reported as it is today.

**A day too long for the column.** Drawn at 18pt an hour; the day column moves to page two
and `crowded` says so — Scenario 4b. Rare, and the rule that a half-hour meeting shows its
name is kept rather than traded for it.

**No second-sheet stories at all.** No sheet, and no link to one anywhere — Scenario 2.

## Out of scope

- The hybrid of Magazine's hero over Broadsheet's columns. Offered, not chosen.
- The routine's copy of the brief is the owner's to update; the new text is handed over, and
  once it is in, the copy is compared with `JOB.md` through the routines API.
- The article pages' fallback summary (`.lede`), which escapes a KW summary the same way.
  Seen, not changed here: the article pages are a non-goal.
