# Requirements — FEAT-260912-9c933f De Tijd articles arrive in full, because Harry logs in by itself — and says when it cannot

## Context & problem

De Tijd is the source most worth having on the morning page, and the hardest to read. Its
headlines are easy. Its articles are not.

Measured on 2026-09-16, from the `harry:c32f0cc` image on the NUC:

- **The feed answers anyone.** `https://www.tijd.be/rss/nieuws.xml` returns 200 to a plain
  request. It is RSS 2.0 with 10 items. Each link is a redirect stub such as
  `https://www.tijd.be/r/t/1/id/10686286`, and no item carries a picture.
- **The article pages do not.** A headless browser gets `403 Blocked`. A headed Chromium —
  a real browser window, drawn on a virtual display — gets 200. That was true of full
  headless Chromium three days earlier, so the edge tightens and will tighten again.
- **A reader who is not logged in gets the first paragraph.** The measured article gave 263
  characters through trafilatura. The page's `<html>` element carries
  `class="paywall-active"`, it shows a "Log in" button (`.trck_sitenav_login`), and its
  structured data says `isAccessibleForFree: false`.
- **Logging in is two form steps on `auth.mediafin.be`**, the login service De Tijd uses:
  an email field (`#username`), then a password. No captcha was shown on the first step.

The old plan was for a person to log in by hand, and for Harry to say when that login
lapsed. On a NUC with no screen, "by hand" means a remote view into a container, and a
chore every few weeks that the page quietly degrades without. **Harry logs in by itself
instead**, with the account's email and password — see [[ADR-260916-b26785]].

A second problem sits underneath. On the NUC, every connector's credentials are already in
that connector's own `.env.local` — iCloud, the tablet, Slack. The container cannot see any
of them: `.dockerignore` keeps every `.env.local` out of the image and nothing mounts one.
So on 2026-09-16 the real stack reported icloud, remarkable and slack as skipped, with their
credentials sitting on the same machine. De Tijd's login would have joined them.

Last, the news connector has to be able to use a De Tijd connector. Connectors load in
alphabetical order today and cannot name each other, so `news` would load before `tijd` and
be handed nothing.

## Goals

- A De Tijd story chosen for the morning page prints its whole article, on the real stack on
  the NUC.
- Harry logs in to De Tijd by itself whenever it has no session or the session has lapsed.
  While the password is right, nobody renews anything.
- When Harry cannot get the article, the story still prints the feed's own summary, and one
  Slack line says which of these it was: the login was refused, De Tijd refused the browser,
  or the browser could not start.
- The real stack reads each connector's own `.env.local`, so a credential lives in exactly
  one file on the NUC.
- A connector can use another connector, and connectors load in the order that needs.

## Non-goals

- **Reading any other paywalled site.** This is De Tijd's connector. A second site is a
  second connector.
- **Pictures for De Tijd stories.** The feed carries none. Harry does not go looking for one
  on the article page.
- **A new tool.** `news_article` already returns an article's text. Nobody asks Claude to
  "log in to De Tijd".
- **Answering a captcha or a one-time code.** Harry says it met one and stops.
- **Changing the dev stack's credentials.** It keeps `.env.dev.local`, so a value tried on
  dev can never be the one the morning page uses.
- **Adding De Tijd to the committed default feeds.** It is only useful with a subscription.
  A machine that has one adds the feed in `.harry/connectors/news/.env.local`.

### Scenario 8: A login page Harry cannot finish

```gherkin
Given a step of the login does not end the way it did on 2026-09-16
When Harry tries to log in
Then it stops at that step, and the whole login gives up within 60 seconds
And does not try again for 6 hours
And the Slack line says what it found
```

**The rule, written down.** Each step has one expected ending, measured on 2026-09-16:

| Step | Harry does | Expected ending |
|---|---|---|
| Consent | Clicks "Optionele cookies weigeren" if it is shown | — |
| Open the login | Clicks `.trck_sitenav_login` on `https://www.tijd.be/` | `auth.mediafin.be/u/login/identifier` with `#username` |
| Email | Fills `#username`, clicks `button[name=action][value=default]` | `/u/login/password` with `#password` |
| Password | Fills `#password`, clicks the same button — the password is **sent** from here | back on `www.tijd.be`, past `login-redirect.html`, or `#error-element-password` |

Nothing is typed on a page that is not `auth.mediafin.be`.

Anything else is a failure, and the Slack line says which of four it was:

- **refused** — an error on `#error-element-password` or `#error-element-username`.
- **challenge** — the page carries a captcha: an element with `data-captcha-provider`, or a
  frame whose address contains `captcha`, `challenges` or `arkoselabs`. These markers are
  Auth0's documented ones. Nobody has seen De Tijd show one, so their fixture is made up
  and says so.
- **login stalled** — before the password was sent, an ending did not arrive in time: slow or
  changed, nobody can tell yet. Nothing was refused, so this waits **15 minutes**, not 6 hours.
- **login page** — after the password was sent, neither ending arrived: most likely a step the
  table does not have, such as a code. This waits 6 hours.

```gherkin
Given De Tijd's login page does not answer at all
When Harry tries to log in
Then the Slack line says the login page did not answer
And Harry waits 15 minutes before trying again, not 6 hours — nothing was refused, so nothing counts towards a lockout
```

### Scenario 9: The browser cannot start

### Scenario 10: On the NUC, the morning page carries a De Tijd story in full

```gherkin
Given the real stack is deployed with the tijd connector loaded
When a page is built through scripts/call_tool.py with one De Tijd story chosen and deliver=false
And the harry-data volume held no De Tijd session before the call, so Harry's own login code ran against the real page

```

## When it degrades

Every failure here costs De Tijd's article text and nothing else. The headline is still a
candidate, and the story still prints with the feed's summary. VRT NWS, BBC News and the
rest of the page never notice.

| What happened | What the page shows | What reaches Slack |
|---|---|---|
| `tijd` is not configured (no EMAIL or PASSWORD) | De Tijd stories print their summary | `De Tijd: De Tijd answered 403` — the news connector's existing line, once per 24 hours. And, on every start, the existing line that Harry started without the tijd connector |
| The session lapsed, and logging in works | The whole article | Nothing. The log says how long the old session lasted |
| The email or password was refused | Summary | One line naming De Tijd and the refusal. No new attempt for 6 hours |
| A captcha, or a step after the password was sent that Harry does not know | Summary | One line saying which. No new attempt for 6 hours |
| A login that stalled before the password was sent | Summary | One line naming the step. No new attempt for 15 minutes |
| The login page did not answer | Summary | One line saying so. No new attempt for 15 minutes |
| De Tijd refused the browser (403) | Summary | One line saying it was the browser, not the login |
| The browser could not start, or stopped mid-page | Summary | One line saying so |
| The article did not load within 30 seconds | Summary | One line saying so |
| De Tijd answered an error for the article, such as 404 | Summary | One line with the status. No login is attempted |
| Logged in, and the article still shows the paywall | Summary | One line suggesting the subscription |
| The saved session is not valid JSON, or the browser refuses it | The whole article, after a fresh login | Nothing. The log says the old session was set aside |
| A read has not finished after 150 seconds | Summary | One line saying Harry stopped waiting. Every De Tijd article after it is answered at once, with one line, until the stuck read comes back |
| The browser or the network fails part-way through a login | Summary | One line naming the step. 15 minutes before the password was sent, 6 hours after it |
| The session cannot be written to `/data/tijd` | The whole article | One line saying so: every article will log in again until it is fixed |
| The feed itself is down | No De Tijd headlines | The news connector's existing line |

**The tijd connector says its own faults.** It raises the Slack line, not the news connector,
for three reasons. It owns the credential, so the line comes from the thing to fix. Its own
context scrubs its declared secrets — `EMAIL` and `PASSWORD` — from every line it sends; a
line raised by news would scrub news's secrets, and news has none. And the answer it hands
news says the fault was already reported, so news does not say it a second time.

**One line per reason, per 24 hours.** The alert key is the reason's name — `refused`,
`challenge`, `login-stalled`, `login-page`, `login-broke`, `login-broke-sent`,
`login-unreachable`, `blocked`, `browser`, `timeout`, `page`, `paywall`, `session`, `stuck`,
`busy` — so five articles failing for one reason are one line, and a refusal after a
browser fault is a second line rather than a silenced one. **Every reason is a fixed
sentence.** None carries an exception's text, a URL's query string, the email or the
password.

The news connector's own article alert is unchanged: `article:<feed>`, one line per source
per 24 hours, for a page it fetched itself or a page with no prose in it. A De Tijd page that
tijd read in full and that turns out to be a video still gets that line.

**The 6-hour wait lives in memory.** A restart clears it, and a restart is also how a fixed
password takes effect. De Tijd's login service blocks an account after repeated failures, so
Harry must never retry a refused login on every article.

## Out of scope

- A remote view (noVNC or similar) for logging in by hand. Rejected in [[ADR-260916-b26785]].
- A history of session lengths. `logged-in-at` holds one time, the latest login; the log line is the only record of how long each session lasted.
- Pictures, bylines or dates scraped from the article page.
- Any change to how VRT NWS or BBC News articles are fetched.
- Committing a logged-in page as a test fixture. The repository is on GitHub; a paid article's
  text and an account's details stay off it. Fixtures of logged-in pages are reduced to the
  markers the code reads, with placeholder prose.

## Open questions

- How long a De Tijd session lasts. The log line in Scenario 5 answers it over time.
